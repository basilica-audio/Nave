# Bundled impulse responses — provenance and licences

Nave ships **nine cabinet impulse responses**, and every one of them was
generated by this repository rather than recorded by anyone.

> ## These are models, not captures
>
> Nothing in this folder is a recording of any cabinet, speaker or microphone.
> Each file is computed by
> [`tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py`](../../tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py) from a
> documented analytical model — a cascade of standard analog-prototype filter
> sections plus reflection taps derived from physical path lengths — and voiced
> from the well-characterised behaviour of moving-coil loudspeakers in boxes.
>
> Issue #33 made unambiguous labelling a binding condition on ever shipping
> synthetic IRs. That is why **every filename begins with `modelled_`**, why
> every display name begins with **"Modelled"**, and why
> `tests/FactoryIrLibraryTests.cpp` asserts both rather than leaving them to
> review. None of these may be described as a capture of a real cabinet, and
> none of them is named after a real cabinet, speaker or microphone.

## Why generated rather than sourced

The licensing bar from the #1 scope decision was "beyond doubt": CC0 or public
domain, from a verified source, with the licence file committed. Very little
clears it. A cabinet capture carries rights from the cabinet, from the
microphone and from whoever pressed record; the popular free packs carry unclear
or non-redistributable terms, mirrors routinely misstate them, and "free to
download" is not a licence at all.

Generating the set removes the question instead of answering it. There is no
third-party recording inside these files, so there is nothing to trace, nothing
to dispute and no licensor to find. The generator is committed alongside the
audio: re-run it, get byte-identical output, compare the SHA-256 recorded in
[`manifest.json`](manifest.json). **Reproducibility is the provenance.**

### Guitar cabinets

**Modelled 4x12 Ceramic Cone** (`guitar-412-cone`) — a sealed 4x12 with a
ceramic-magnet 12" voicing, generic cardioid dynamic on the dust cap at 2.5 cm.
An under-damped second-order box alignment at 82 Hz, the boxy dip at 420 Hz, the
pair of cone-breakup peaks at 1.65 kHz and 2.45 kHz with the null between them
at 3.3 kHz, then a 24 dB/oct cliff from 5.2 kHz. Baffle-edge and floor
reflections at 22 cm and 48 cm of excess path.

**Modelled 4x12 Ceramic Edge** (`guitar-412-edge`) — the same cabinet with the
microphone at the cone edge and angled: a −7 dB high-shelf from 3 kHz plus an
extra low-pass pole for the cone's directivity, 2 dB less of the 2.45 kHz
presence peak, and +2 dB at 250 Hz. The darker half of a cone/edge IR Blend.

**Modelled 4x12 Room 1m** (`guitar-412-room`) — the same cabinet at one metre in
a small tracking room. No proximity rise (a −2 dB low-shelf instead), −2.5 dB of
air absorption above 6 kHz, four early reflections at 65 / 180 / 240 / 330 cm,
and a diffuse tail 14 dB below the direct field, band-limited to 120 Hz – 4 kHz
and decaying with a 110 ms time constant. The noise for that tail comes from an
explicitly-seeded 64-bit LCG (seed `0x4E415645`) implemented in the generator
itself, so the tail is reproducible rather than "some noise".

**Modelled 2x12 Alnico Cone** (`guitar-212-alnico`) — an open-back 2x12 with an
alnico-magnet voicing. The open back radiates as a dipole, so the low end rolls
off at 6 dB/oct (a first-order section at 130 Hz), not 12, with a cancellation
notch at 180 Hz. Earlier, sweeter breakup than ceramic: peaks at 1.9 kHz and
3.1 kHz. The rear wave returning at 55 cm of excess path is the characteristic
open-back hollowness.

**Modelled 1x12 Combo Cone** (`guitar-112-combo`) — a small open-back combo:
first-order dipole roll-off from 160 Hz, a pronounced small-box resonance at
190 Hz that reads as honk rather than weight, and a strong 2.2 kHz breakup peak.

### Bass cabinets

**Modelled 8x10 Cone** (`bass-810-cone`) — a sealed 8x10 stack, dynamic on the
dust cap at 2 cm. Under-damped second-order alignment at 55 Hz, the multi-driver
low-mid dip at 300 Hz, the 10" cone's growl at 700 Hz and 1.5 kHz, breakup null
at 2.6 kHz, 24 dB/oct inductance roll-off from 4 kHz. No tweeter.

**Modelled 8x10 Edge** (`bass-810-edge`) — the same cabinet, microphone at the
cone edge and off-axis: −6 dB high-shelf from 2.5 kHz, one extra low-pass pole,
+2 dB at 220 Hz.

**Modelled 1x15 Vintage** (`bass-115-vintage`) — a ported 1x15 with a dark
vintage voicing and a generic ribbon at 5 cm. Fourth-order vented alignment
tuned around 45 Hz, the 15" cone's early breakup and death above 2.2 kHz, and
the ribbon's strong proximity rise with no presence peak at all. The dark end of
the set.

**Modelled 4x10 Horn** (`bass-410-horn`) — a ported 4x10 with an HF horn on a
passive crossover. Two parallel paths: a woofer path low-passed at 24 dB/oct
from 3 kHz, and a horn path high-passed at 24 dB/oct from 3.5 kHz, at 0.5 gain
and delayed by 4 samples because a horn's acoustic centre sits behind the woofer
baffle plane. That offset is what makes the crossover region comb the way a real
one does. The bright end of the set.

## Verified measurements

Measured by [`tools/ir-synth/verify_irs.py`](../../tools/ir-synth/verify_irs.py)
from the shipped files, not from the code that wrote them, and re-measured
through the plugin's own reader and convolution engine by
`tests/FactoryIrLibraryTests.cpp`.

Every file, without exception:

- is mono, 24-bit PCM, 48 kHz;
- contains **no clipped sample** and no non-finite sample;
- has a **DC offset below 1e-4** and a DC term **at least 60 dB below** the peak
  of its magnitude response (measured range: −69 dB to −126 dB);
- is **normalised so that max |H(f)| = 0 dBFS** to within 0.001 dB, which is the
  criterion that bounds the convolution: no sine at any frequency can leave the
  convolver hotter than it entered. Nave's Energy/Loudness gain modes (see
  `src/dsp/IrLoudness.h`) do the perceptual level-matching on top of that;
- **decays to digital silence** on its last samples, because the generator
  applies a raised-cosine fade rather than truncating — an IR still ringing when
  it is cut convolves as a step discontinuity;
- carries **less than 0.01 % of its energy in its final tenth**.

Per-file peak, RMS, DC, corner frequencies, decay time and the full octave-band
response table are in [`manifest.json`](manifest.json) and reproduced by running:

```
python3 tools/ir-synth/verify_irs.py resources/irs \
    --expect-manifest resources/irs/manifest.json --min-files 9
```

`--expect-manifest` cross-checks every file's SHA-256 against this record, so
the provenance documentation cannot drift away from the audio it describes.

## Regenerating

```
python3 tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py --out-dir resources/irs --models all \
    --manifest resources/irs/manifest.json
```

The generator uses the Python standard library and nothing else — no numpy, no
network access — deliberately, because a generator that needs a pinned
scientific stack to reproduce its output is a weaker reproducibility claim than
one that does not. `python3 tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py --list` prints the model
table.

## Adding to the library

1. Add the model to `tools/ir-synth/cabsynth.py` and re-run it, or verify a
   third-party asset's licence **at the source** and archive the evidence — the
   licence page itself, not a third party's description of it.
2. Re-run `verify_irs.py`; it fails the build on a clipped, DC-offset,
   mis-normalised or undocumented asset.
3. Add a row and a description to this file.
4. If it is generated, keep the `modelled_` prefix. If it is not, it must not
   have one.
